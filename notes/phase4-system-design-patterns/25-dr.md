# Disaster recovery (RPO / RTO)

> Phase 4 · Tags: `reliability`

## 1. The concept
- **RPO** (Recovery Point Objective): how much data are you willing to lose, measured in time. RPO = 5 min means backups every 5 min.
- **RTO** (Recovery Time Objective): how quickly you must be back up. RTO = 1 hour means you must restore in under an hour.

DR tiers, weakest → strongest:
- **Backup & restore**: restore from offsite backups. Hours to days RTO.
- **Pilot light**: minimal standby env, scale up on disaster. Tens of minutes RTO.
- **Warm standby**: scaled-down replica running, can take over. Minutes RTO.
- **Active-active**: multi-region traffic; failover is automatic. Near-zero RTO/RPO.

## 2. The rule / the why
Backups are useless if you can't restore them. RPO/RTO turn "we have backups" into measurable, testable commitments.

## 3. Java-specific behavior
- Application state should be in managed data stores, not on app instances. App instances should be stateless and disposable.
- DB backups (logical + physical), Redis persistence (RDB + AOF), Kafka MirrorMaker for cross-region replication.
- Test restore in a non-prod env on a schedule — not just at incident time.

## 4. System design angle
- RPO drives backup frequency + replication mode (sync vs async).
- RTO drives standby strategy (cold/warm/hot) — cost grows fast.
- Multi-region active-active is expensive and complex; only pay for it when business requires it.
- Don't forget the "out of band" stuff: DNS records, secrets, IAM policies, CI/CD config — back those up too.

## 5. Common mistakes / traps
- Untested backups: "we have them" → discover at restore time they're corrupted.
- All backups in the same region as the primary.
- Long RTO from "we have to rebuild from infrastructure-as-code" — never timed it.
- Forgetting state outside the DB (cache warm-up, in-flight messages, search indices).
- Ignoring data residency / sovereignty when picking DR region.
